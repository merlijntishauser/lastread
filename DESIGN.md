# Design System: Lastread

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "Two Sides"**

The page is drawn as the handover it makes. Every book has two sides: the
Kobo's, where it is read, and Hardcover's, where it is recorded. The Kobo's
side is grey e-ink paper: the book, a big figure of how far it is read,
the cover in greyscale, the Kobo's own dotted line of progress, and the
two switches, *Sync* and *State*. Hardcover's side is white, and there the
cover takes its colour. Between them runs the seam, an ink rule, and on the
seam each row carries its mark: what the next sync does with that book.
A reader who looks down the seam sees the whole handover at once.

The world is the two devices' own. The grey is the Kobo's screen, the
white is the web page that receives the book, the ink rule is the edge
where one hands over to the other. Above everything a dark band is the
tool itself: the name after a red caret, the navigation, what the Kobo and
Hardcover last did, and *Refresh* and *Sync now*. A quiet foot holds who
is signed in, the theme switch, Help and the sentence that says the
project is independent. Red is only for what a sync sends and the button
that sends it; blue is a question for the reader; a green tick is up to
date; ink is a failure and the reader's chosen filter. Everything else is
ink and grey.

It turns down the usual look for this kind of tool: a neutral list with
coloured status badges scattered across it. Status lives in one place, on
the seam, as a drawn mark with plain words beside it. Covers are the only
pictures, and the tool fetches them itself. The one authored motion is
kept from the earlier proofreading direction: when a value changes, the
old one is struck through and the new one writes in after a caret.

**The Mac app** (`packaging/macos/`) is drawn in Two Sides as well: the
dark band across the top of every pane, the Kobo's grey and Hardcover's
white with the ink seam between them and each book's mark on the seam as a
disc, in Books, Overview and Covers alike. Titles and the percentage are
Literata, in the window and the widget; controls and the words around them
are the system font, as a Mac app's are. Its colours are the page's tokens;
the few `mac-` tokens are only what macOS asks for beyond them (a red that
holds on a chosen row, the fill of a native button that sends, the stronger
values Increase Contrast takes). Its rules are the *(Mac app)* sections of
Components.

The site (`site/`) is a world of its own, kept apart from the tool's and
the Mac app's: the claim and the download, then one row of the tool's
Books page drawn at full size with the proof mark on it, on a white
ground by day and a near-black one at night, and under it the three the
sync runs between, the Kobo, your computer and Hardcover, one card each,
with photographs of people reading as cards too. It keeps its two faces
from its own font files, the page's marks, its one motion and its theme
switch, and the drawn row keeps the Galley Proof's colours. Its tokens
carry the `site-` prefix, the `site-sheet-` set and the three gradient
families, and its rules are under *The site* in Colors and Components.

**Key Characteristics:**
- Two sides per book: the Kobo's on grey paper (`ground`), Hardcover's on white (`sheet`); the cover grey on one, in colour on the other.
- The seam: a 2px ink rule between the sides, holding each row's mark as a 2rem disc.
- Five discs on the seam: red filled with a caret (the next sync changes this), white with a green tick ring (up to date), blue filled with a query (needs a match), grey with a dash (not syncing, or nothing to send yet), white with an ink square (failed).
- How far a book is read, set large in Literata at 700, beside its cover.
- A dark band across the top holds the tool; a quiet foot closes the page.
- Two typefaces with one job each: Literata for book titles, page heads and the percentage; Atkinson Hyperlegible Next for everything else.
- Red only for what a sync sends: *Sync now* is red only when the sync is live, and outlined in a dry run.
- Light and dark share every role; WCAG 2.2 AA in both.
- One authored motion: the strike draws, the new value writes in.

### Named Rules
**The One Red Rule.** Red fills only *Sync now* while the sync is live,
and *Go live*, and marks only what a sync sends. In a dry run *Sync now*
is outlined in the band's ink like *Refresh*, because nothing goes to
Hardcover. Every other filled button is an ink fill (*Start using
Lastread*, *Connect to Hardcover*, *I have approved it*, *Use*, a
confirmation, *Check and save*). Blue is only for a question for the
reader or the step they are on; a label that states a fact (*New*,
*Admin*) is grey. A removing action is ink, never red. The site has no
red fill at all: its one filled button is the card's reverse, and the
sheet carries red as a mark only.

**The Seam Rule.** A row's status is said once, on the seam, as a disc
with the mark in it and the words beside it on Hardcover's side. The
legend, folded behind *What the marks mean*, holds every mark the seam can
carry, drawn and coloured the same, with the words of the page's strings:
*Next sync changes this*, *Nothing to send yet*, *Up to date*, *Kept as on
Hardcover*, *Needs you*, *Failed*, *Not syncing*. Help explains each one
under *Hardcover status*. A new mark is not used until it is in both.

**The Two Lights Rule.** Every colour has a light and a dark value with
the same role. A colour that exists in one theme only is a mistake. In
dark the red and blue are lighter and fills carry dark words
(`on-fill-dark`); the band's colours are the same in both. On the site
the gradient and photo cards are the same in both lights, a card does not
change with the room; the ground, the words, the quiet card, the download
card and the sheet swap, the download card from ink to white, so that the
action stays the strongest thing on the ground in each light.

**The Legible Rule.** Words stand at 4.5:1 or more on whatever they are
on, the edge of a field and every mark at 3:1 (WCAG 2.2 AA), in both
themes. `tests/test_design_tokens.py` checks the pairs from `kobo.css`.
The site's pairs are given under *The site* above; on a card the words
stand on the dark end of the gradient, never on the bright stop.

## Typography

**Display Font:** Atkinson Hyperlegible Next (with -apple-system, Segoe UI, sans-serif)
**Body Font:** Atkinson Hyperlegible Next
**Book Font:** Literata (with Georgia, Times New Roman, serif), for book titles, page heads and the percentage
**Label/Mono Font:** the system monospace, for the sign-in code, tokens and short technical values

**Character:** Literata was made for reading books on a screen, so the
books, the page's name and how far each is read are set in a reading
face. Atkinson Hyperlegible Next was made so that letters and figures are
told apart at a glance, which is what a dense row of facts, switches and
statuses asks for; it is firm at 800 for the few sans headings and plain
at 13px in a row. Both are served by the tool from its own static files
(latin and latin-ext, Atkinson in roman and italic), under the SIL Open
Font Licence, weights 400 to 800 on the `wght` axis, Literata with optical
sizing on. Schibsted Grotesk and Source Serif 4 stay in the fonts folder
and are not loaded by the page. The Mac app sets its titles and the
percentage in Literata from the page's own files, which its build copies
into the app and into the widget: the latin subset, with latin-ext behind
it as a cascade for the letters only it has (registered side by side,
macOS chose latin-ext, which holds no lowercase, and set titles in the
system font), at the weight on the `wght` axis and `opsz` at the point
size; without the files, the serif macOS has. Everything else in the app
is the system font.

### Hierarchy
- **Display** (Atkinson, 800, 1.3rem, 1.35rem from 1024px, 1.05, -0.015em): the name in the band, once, after the red caret.

### Named Rules
**The Reading Face Rule.** Literata is for what is read as a book: book
titles in the list, at the top of Details and under a large cover, a
page's head, and the percentage. The name, sans headings, buttons, labels
and every fact are Atkinson Hyperlegible Next. The Mac app keeps the rule
in its own terms: Literata for book titles, the percentage and the heads
of Overview's two sides, the system font for everything else. The site's
drawn row keeps its own title face (`site-sheet-title`).

**The Figures Rule.** Tabular figures go only where numbers stand in
columns or are compared: the percentage, the facts under a gauge

of the list's count, Getting started's tally and step numbers, the count
on *Sync now*, and code. Never on the root or on running text.
`tests/test_design_tokens.py` holds this.

## Elevation & Depth

Flat paper. The sides are told apart by tone and the seam, not by
shadow. Cards and the white cards above the list lie on the grey with a
1px Rule and, in light only, a faint fall of shadow; in dark there is none
and nothing is lost. Book cards have no shadow, and from 1024px the list
is not a sheet at all but the two tones and the seam. Real depth is kept
for things physically on top: a cover and a dialog. On the site the
gradient and photo cards and the sheet lie on the ground with one soft
fall of shadow by day and none at night; the quiet cards have none, the
two icons cast a drop shadow on their card, and the cover in the sheet
lies on it with the cover shadow.

### Shadow Vocabulary
- **Lift, light only** (`box-shadow: 0 1px 0 #bdbcb4, 0 18px 40px -30px rgba(28, 28, 26, 0.35)`): under cards, and under Getting started and *Needs you* on a desk. `none` in dark.
- **Cover** (`box-shadow: 0 1px 2px rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.28), 0 3px 8px rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.1)`): a book cover lying on the Kobo's side, in both lights.
- **Small cover** (`box-shadow: 0 1px 2px rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.25)`): the 32px colour cover on Hardcover's side.
- **Dialog** (`box-shadow: 0 24px 64px rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.35)`): Help and Details, over `rgba(10, 12, 14, 0.6)`. The cover lightbox uses `0 24px 64px rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.55)` over `rgba(10, 12, 14, 0.88)`.
- **Disc ring** (`box-shadow: inset 0 0 0 1.5px currentColor`): the ring of a white disc on the seam (green for up to date, ink for failed); a grey disc has `inset 0 0 0 1px var(--edge)`. A ring, not a shadow.
- **Site lift, light only** (`box-shadow: 0 24px 48px -28px rgba(23, 25, 28, 0.45)`): under the site's gradient and photo cards and its sheet; `none` at night and on the quiet cards. The app icon casts `drop-shadow(0 10px 18px rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.3))` on the download card and `drop-shadow(0 18px 30px rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.35))` on the cobalt card.

### Named Rules
**The Flat Paper Rule.** Surfaces are told apart by tone and a rule. A
shadow is never needed to read the page; the dark theme proves it.

## Shapes

Soft-cornered paper with three round things. Cards, book cards and
dialogs are 4px; buttons, fields, selects, *Details*, the theme switch's
frame and navigation items are 6px; small inner parts (a tag, a
theme-switch button) are 3px, the reversed *Failed* words, the small cover's fore-edge and the large cover in the lightbox 2px (`small`). The round
things each mean one kind of thing: the marks on the seam are 2rem discs,
the filter chips are pills, and the step numbers of Getting started sit in
1.6rem circles. Covers are 1px at the spine and 3px at the fore-edge (the
small one on Hardcover's side 1px and 2px), kept because a book's spine is
square. The focus ring is a 2.5px Question Blue outline 2px off the
control, in the band's blue on the band, the bar under the thumb and the
lightbox.

The site has its cards' own rounding instead: 18px on every card and on
the sheet, 12px on the button and the theme switch (9px on its three
buttons), 10px on a screenshot inside its card, 8px on a code chip;
inside the sheet 4px on the drawn switches and *Details*
(`site-sheet-ctl`), and the cover's 1px spine and 3px fore-edge; its
focus ring is 3px with 4px corners.

The Mac app's corners are in points: 4pt on a white sheet, 8pt on the
white box above the list and on the last sync on Overview, 7pt on what you
operate (the band's buttons, the app's own buttons, the slip of *Hold* and
*Show* on the seam), 2pt on a cover, a caption and a row's word. Its discs
are the page's: 24pt on the list's seam, 40pt on Overview's chain and its
hop, 30pt for the tip on Covers' seam, 20pt on a cover the next sync
changes, at full size.

Lines carry the structure. Borders are 1px; the edge of a control is
1.5px. The one heavy line is the seam, a 2px ink rule
(`--seam-w`): down the list between the sides on a desk, across each book
card on a phone, and over the end of the list on a phone and tablet. Help
keeps a 3px double rule under its title bar. The site has no rules
between cards: they are told apart by colour. Its 1px lines are the
sheet's border and the rules inside it (under the rail, under the head
row, over the caption, with the 3px double rule where its margin begins),
and the line between the rows of the other tools' table.

**The Round Means Something Rule.** A round shape on the page is one of
three: a mark's disc on the seam, a filter chip (a pill), or a step
number's circle. Buttons, fields, switches and tags stay square-cornered
at 6px or 3px. The site's largest corner on anything you operate is 12px.

### Motion
One authored moment, kept from the proofreading direction. When the list
loads, and whenever a row is redrawn, the strike draws across the old
value (0.5s) and the new value writes in from the left (0.6s, 0.2s
later), both on an expo ease-out (`cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)`), each
row 70ms after the one above (up to the thirteenth). The rest is
functional: coming back to a row washes the Kobo's side of it Blue Wash
for 1.8s; a large cover fades and grows in for 0.18s; while a sync runs,
its red gauge in the band grows by `transform: scaleX` (0.4s, the same
expo ease-out), or sweeps across by `translateX` (1.1s, ease-in-out, back
and forth) while the count is not known yet; the chevrons of *Paste a
token instead* turn in 0.2s. Under `prefers-reduced-motion` all of them
stop: the row you came back to keeps a steady wash, and the sweeping gauge
becomes a faint full line instead of moving.

One exception, approved by the reader, for the Mac app's *Covers* pane
alone: a second authored motion, the drift of the run and the lift. The
rows move left at 7, 11 and 17pt a second (back, middle, front, at full
size, scaled with the run), the front row one cover in about six seconds.
A row eases to a stop and back on an exponential ease (0.18s time
constant) while a cover in it is lifted, while the keyboard is in the run,
and while the large cover or the spread is open; a move by hand holds all
three rows for 3s. The lift is 1.12x on the expo ease-out over 0.25s; the
large cover and the spread come and go in 0.18s, fading and growing from 0.96. Under
Reduce motion, and whenever the window is not visible, the run stands
still and moves only by hand; under Reduce motion a lifted cover does not
grow and the large cover and the spread appear without moving. Nowhere else does
anything drift or lift.

The site has one of its own, once on load: the download card, the sheet
and the three story cards rise into place, 18px up from transparent, in
0.7s on the expo ease-out, 60ms apart in page order; then in the sheet's
row the strike draws across the old value in 0.5s at 0.9s and the new
value writes in from the left in 0.6s at 1.1s, the tool's own motion at
the tool's own timing, after the sheet has landed. The three story cards
stand still once landed. Hover thickens a link's underline and underlines
the button; nothing else moves. Under `prefers-reduced-motion` the cards
and the sheet stand in place, the strike and write-in are off, and the
smooth scroll to an in-page link is off too.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
- **Do** put a book's status on the seam, as one 2rem disc with its mark, and its words beside it on Hardcover's side.
- **Do** strike the old value in red and write the new value after a red caret when the next sync changes something.
- **Do** fill red only *Sync now* while the sync is live and *Go live*; outline *Sync now* in a dry run, and give every other filled button the ink fill.
- **Do** keep blue for a question for the reader, or the step they are on.
- **Do** fill the chosen filter chip, the pressed theme button and any other chosen state in ink.
- **Do** add any new mark to the legend and to Help's *Hardcover status*, drawn and coloured the same, before it appears on the seam.
- **Do** show the cover in grey on the Kobo's side and in colour on Hardcover's.
- **Do** set book titles, page heads and the percentage in Literata, and everything else in Atkinson Hyperlegible Next.
- **Do** put `tabular-nums` only on numbers that stand in columns or are compared: the percentage, the facts, chip counts, the Filter count, the end of the list's count, Getting started's tally and step numbers, code.
- **Do** use 4px for cards, book cards and dialogs, 6px for what you operate, 3px for small inner parts, and the 2px ink seam as the one heavy line.
- **Do** make every control 2.75rem below 1024px and under a finger.
- **Do** give every new colour a light and a dark value with the same role, and check it: 4.5:1 for words, 3:1 for field edges and marks; on the band use the band's own mark colours.
- **Do** make every action work without JavaScript, and stop every motion under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- **Do** draw a missing cover as the crossed box.
- **Do** keep the site's cards the same in both lights, the words on the dark end of the gradient; only the download card and the sheet swap, the card ink by day and white at night.
- **Do** draw the site's marks as the page's caret, tick, circled query and dash, inline SVG: in the words' colour on a card, in the sheet's own red, green, blue and faint on the sheet.

### Don't:
- **Don't** scatter status as coloured dots or badges across a row; a book's status is its one disc on the seam, with plain words beside it.
- **Don't** use red for decoration, for a warning, or for a button that removes something; a removing action is ink, underlined.
- **Don't** use pill shapes for buttons, tags, fields or switches; the pill is the filter chip's, and the disc is the seam's.
- **Don't** set `tabular-nums` on the root or on running text.
- **Don't** set anything but a book title, a page head or the percentage in Literata.
- **Don't** load Schibsted Grotesk or Source Serif 4 on the page; the Mac app takes Literata from the page's own files.
- **Don't** rely on a shadow to separate surfaces; the dark theme has none.
- **Don't** set labels in capitals or track them out.
- **Don't** add a second authored motion; the strike and the write-in are the one. The Mac app's *Covers* pane is the one approved exception: the drift and the lift, standing still under Reduce motion and when not visible. The site's is its own one: the download card, the sheet and the story cards rise once on load and the strike and write-in follow in the sheet's row, standing still under reduced motion.
- **Don't** load a font, a script, a style or a picture from elsewhere; the tool serves everything, the two fonts and the book covers included, and the site bundles its fonts and its four photographs. The site's one request out is the latest release's version, with JavaScript; without it the download link stands.
- **Don't** put a kicker or an eyebrow above a heading, on the page or on the site, or a hairline between the site's cards; colour parts them. The site sheet's rules stay inside the sheet.
